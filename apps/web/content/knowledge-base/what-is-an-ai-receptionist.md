An AI receptionist is software that answers your business phone with a natural-sounding voice, works out what the caller wants and acts on it: booking an appointment, taking an order, answering a question or passing the call to a person. It picks up on the first ring, works around the clock and can take more than one call at a time. For a small business, it covers the calls that would otherwise ring out while you and your staff are busy with the customers in front of you.

## What happens on a call

Picture a Thursday afternoon at a salon. Every stylist has a client in the chair and the phone rings. Here is what an AI receptionist does with that call:

1. It answers on the first ring with your greeting.
2. It matches the caller's number to a regular who books color every eight weeks.
3. The caller asks to move Saturday's appointment. The agent checks the live calendar, offers two open times with the same stylist, and the caller picks one.
4. It updates the booking, sends a confirmation text, and saves a short summary and transcript for the front desk.

Nobody put down the scissors. The same flow works for a restaurant taking a pickup order, a dental office moving a cleaning, or an HVAC company booking a no-heat call for first thing tomorrow.

## AI receptionist, AI virtual receptionist and answering service

You will see several terms for overlapping products. It helps to pin them down before you compare quotes.

- **AI receptionist** and **AI virtual receptionist** usually mean the same thing: a voice agent that answers your line. "Virtual receptionist" on its own used to mean a remote human team, so check which one a vendor is selling.
- **Answering service** usually means people in a call center who take messages from a script. Some now add AI to screen or transcribe calls.
- **IVR** (interactive voice response) is the "press 1 for hours" phone menu. It routes calls and rarely finishes a task.
- **Voicemail** records a message and leaves all the work to you.

The practical test is whether the call ends with the job done (booked, ordered, rescheduled) or with a message someone has to return. We compare the two approaches side by side in [AI receptionist vs. answering service](/insights/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service).

## How an AI receptionist works

Four pieces run in a loop for every turn of the conversation:

- **Speech recognition** turns the caller's audio into text as they talk.
- **A language model** reads the conversation and decides the next step, inside rules you set.
- **Tools and integrations** let it act: check open slots, read the menu, create a booking, send a text.
- **Speech synthesis** turns the reply into a voice and plays it back.

The phone side connects through your carrier, usually by forwarding the number you already have. Of the four pieces, integrations matter most to you as an owner. An agent that cannot see your calendar can only take messages. ## What it handles well

### Booking, rescheduling and cancellations

This is the core job. When the agent reads and writes the same calendar your staff uses, it only offers real openings and the booking shows up instantly. It can apply your rules: deposits for long services, buffer time between appointments, which staff member does which service.

### Orders and reservations

For restaurants, the agent can take a takeout order with modifiers ("no onions, extra salsa"), quote a pickup time and take a reservation with party size and notes such as a birthday or a high chair. The order should land in the same kitchen queue as orders taken at the counter.

### Repeat questions

Hours, parking, prices, whether you take walk-ins, what to bring to a first visit. These calls are short and constant, and they interrupt staff more than any other kind.

### After hours and overflow

It answers at 9 PM and during the lunch rush when every line is busy. A plumbing company can have it collect the address and the problem, flag an emergency and reach the on-call tech.

### Follow-up

Callers who did not book can get a follow-up text. Good systems let you approve those messages before they go out.

## Where it falls short

Be clear-eyed about the limits before you switch one on.

- **Judgment calls.** A refund dispute, a complaint about a bad haircut or a billing argument needs a person. Set rules that send these to staff.
- **Clinical or legal advice.** A clinic's agent can book and move appointments. It should not judge whether a symptom is urgent beyond a scripted line such as "If this is an emergency, hang up and call 911."
- **Data it can't see.** If your prices, stock or hours live in a spreadsheet nobody updates, the agent will repeat stale answers with total confidence.
- **Messy audio.** Speakerphones in cars, loud rooms and weak connections still cause mistakes, most often with names, spellings and long numbers.
- **Some callers want a person.** Make "talk to someone" fast and easy. Forcing people through the agent costs more goodwill than it saves.

## AI receptionist for small business: what changes by industry

The technology is the same everywhere. The vocabulary, rules and integrations differ, and those decide whether the agent is useful.

- **Salons and spas.** Service lengths, stylist preferences, deposits and no-show policies. "A balayage with Maya" has to map to the right service, duration and person.
- **Restaurants.** Seating times the floor can actually handle, menu modifiers, allergies and realistic pickup times during a rush.
- **Clinics and dental offices.** New-patient intake, forms to send, insurance questions to route to staff and strict limits on what the agent may say.
- **Trades.** Triage comes first. A no-heat call in January is urgent. A quote for a new water heater can wait until morning. The agent needs the address, the problem and a way to reach whoever is on call.
- **Retail.** Where an order is, tracking and delivery dates, cancellations, size or address changes before shipping, and returns and exchanges. ## How pricing works

AI receptionist pricing tends to follow one of four models.

- **Per minute.** You pay for the time the agent spends on calls. Fair when volume is low, harder to predict when it spikes.
- **Per call or per conversation.** Each answered call costs a set amount, sometimes with a length cap. A 30-second hours question costs the same as a ten-minute booking.
- **Flat monthly with an allowance.** A fixed fee covers a set number of calls or minutes. Predictable until you pass the cap and overage rates kick in.
- **Bundled with software.** Some point-of-sale or booking systems include the receptionist in a plan. Sonorch, for example, publishes POS plans from $99 a month for up to 5 staff that include the AI receptionist with unlimited calls, plus a free tier for cash and gift cards.

To compare, build a monthly total from your own call logs. Say you get 600 calls a month averaging 2.5 minutes each. That is 1,500 minutes. Multiply by any per-minute rate you are quoted, then add setup fees, phone number fees, integration add-ons and likely overage. Put that total next to the flat plans. Then count what comes back. If the agent books two appointments a week that would have gone to voicemail and never been returned, multiply two by your average ticket and by four weeks. That is the number to weigh against the bill.

## How to evaluate an AI receptionist

### Call it yourself, more than once

Run a test script: book, reschedule, cancel, ask a price, ask something off-topic, interrupt it mid-sentence, mumble a name, ask for a person. Note where it stumbles and how it recovers.

### Check how deep the integration goes

Ask whether it writes bookings straight to your live calendar or sends you a request to enter by hand. The first saves time. The second just moves the typing to a different moment.

### Watch the handoff

Ask how a live transfer works, what your staff member hears before picking up and what happens if nobody answers.

### Read the transcripts

Every call should leave a summary and transcript you can search. That is how you catch mistakes early and tune the agent over the first few weeks.

## Questions to ask a vendor

- What counts toward billing: minutes, calls or both? What happens past the cap?
- Does it read and write my calendar, menu or POS directly?
- Can it take deposits, and who handles the card data?
- How do I change hours, policies and the greeting, and how fast do changes take effect?
- How does it transfer to a person, and can I set rules by time of day or reason for the call?
- Where are recordings and transcripts stored, who on my team can see them, and can I delete them?
- Can I keep my current phone number?
- Which languages can it handle on a live call?
