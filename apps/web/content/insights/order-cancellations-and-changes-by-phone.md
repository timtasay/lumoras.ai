An order cancellation or change is easy before the order is packed and hard after it ships. Check the order's status the moment the caller asks, apply written rules for what can change and until when, confirm the change in writing, and send any refund back to the original payment method. Routine requests can be finished on the call, by your team or an AI voice agent, and the rest go to a person.

## The window before fulfillment

Every order passes a point where changing it stops being a quick edit. Before that point, a cancellation is a click and a size swap is a new line on the order. After it, the same request becomes a return, an exchange or a call to the carrier.

Where that point sits depends on how you ship: when picking starts, when the label prints, or when the carrier scans the parcel. Pick one as your cutoff and use it everywhere: on your website, in your confirmation email and in what your phone team or voice agent says.

That makes speed matter. A caller who reaches you at 9:05 can often be helped. Call them back at 11:00 and the order may already be packed.

## Write the rules down

Your team and any voice agent need the same rules, written as decisions they can apply. A simple table covers most requests:

| Request | Before it's packed | After it ships |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cancel the whole order | Cancel and refund | Treat as a return |
| Cancel one item | Remove it and refund that item | Treat as a return |
| Swap size or color | Swap if the new one is in stock | Treat as an exchange |
| Fix the address | Small fixes on the call; bigger changes to a person | Pass to a person |
| Faster shipping | Upgrade and collect the difference | Usually not possible |

Then add what the table leaves out: only verified callers can make changes, which items can't be cancelled (such as personalized pieces), and what happens to shipping charges on a partial cancellation.

## Size and color swaps

This is the most common change for clothing and shoe stores. Handle it in four steps:

1. Confirm which item the caller means.
2. Check stock for the exact variant they want.
3. If the price differs, say so and handle the difference under your rules.
4. Read the change back in full: "So that's the black shirt in large, replacing the navy in medium."

If the new size is out of stock, offer the real options: keep the original, cancel that item, or wait for a restock if you can give a date.

## Address fixes

Address changes need the most care, because a new address right after purchase is a common fraud pattern. A good rule: allow small corrections on the call, such as a missing apartment number or a typo in the street name, once the caller is verified. Send bigger changes, like a different city or a new recipient, to a person who can check the order more closely.

Once a parcel has shipped, redirecting it isn't always possible, so pass those calls to your team.

## Shipping upgrades

A caller who needs the order sooner may want faster shipping. Check whether the order can still make today's cutoff for the faster service, quote the difference, and collect it the way your store normally does. If it still can't arrive in time, say so plainly and let the caller decide whether to keep it or cancel.

## Confirm everything in writing

Every change should end with a written confirmation sent to the contact details on the order, never to a new email or number the caller gives on the spot. It should say:

- what changed, item by item
- the new order total
- any refund amount and where it is going
- the new delivery estimate, if it moved

Then add a note to the order, so the next person who opens it sees what happened.

## Refunds go back to the original payment

Refund a cancellation to the payment method used for the order. It is the simplest rule to explain and the hardest to abuse. Requests to send a refund to a different card, or to issue it as cash or a gift card, should go to a person.

Tell the caller when the refund was issued and that it can take a few business days to appear, depending on their bank. For a partial cancellation, refund only the cancelled item, plus any shipping your policy says comes back with it.

## What to escalate

Set clear lines for when a request leaves the routine path:

- the order has already shipped and the caller needs the address changed
- a large change to the shipping address or recipient
- a refund to a different payment method
- a caller who fails verification, or a request that looks like fraud
- an upset caller, or a repeat call about the same order
