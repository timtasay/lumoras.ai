Starting a return or exchange by phone takes four steps: find the order, check each item against your return policy, send a return label or instructions, and record whether the customer wants a refund, store credit or a different size. With your policy written as clear rules, your team or an AI voice agent can finish most of these calls in a few minutes, and anything outside the policy goes to a person.

## Why people call about returns

Most stores have a returns page or portal, and people still call. The reasons are practical:

- They checked out as a guest and can't find a way in.
- The item was a gift, and they don't have the order number.
- They aren't sure whether the item can be returned at all.
- They want a different size without waiting for a refund first.
- It arrived damaged, or it isn't what they ordered.

A clear answer keeps a return from turning into a complaint, and an exchange keeps the sale.

## Write your policy as rules

A policy written for shoppers is often too loose for a phone script. Turn it into rules that get applied the same way every time:

- **The window.** How many days, and counted from when: the order date or the delivery date.
- **Condition.** Unworn, unwashed, tags attached, original packaging.
- **Excluded items.** Final sale, personalized or made-to-order pieces, gift cards, and items you can't resell for hygiene reasons.
- **Return shipping.** Free, a flat fee deducted from the refund, or free for exchanges only.
- **Refund method.** Original payment, store credit, or the customer's choice.
- **Gift returns.** Usually store credit for the recipient.

Here is an example rule, written to be applied: "Items can be returned within 30 days of delivery if unworn with tags attached. Final-sale items can't be returned. Exchanges ship free." Use your own terms.

## Check eligibility on the call

With the rules written down, the check takes a minute:

1. **Verify the caller** with the order number plus the email, phone number or zip code on the order.
2. **Pick the items.** Ask which items are coming back, since many returns are partial.
3. **Check the window.** As an example, an order delivered on September 2 with a 30-day window can be returned until October 2.
4. **Check exclusions.** Is any item marked final sale or personalized?
5. **Ask the reason.** Too small, too big, damaged, wrong item. It decides the next step.
6. **Ask about condition.** "Is it unworn, with the tags still on?"

If every answer passes, the return can start on the call.

## Send the return label

Send the label or return instructions by email or text, to the contact details already on the order. Include:

- the order number and the items being returned
- the label itself, or a code for drop-off if your shipping setup uses one
- where to drop it off and by when
- any return fee, stated before the label is sent

Then tell the caller how they will hear about the refund, credit or exchange.

## Exchanges for another size

For clothing and shoes, an exchange is often what the caller wanted all along. Check stock for the exact size and color first. Then follow your rule for timing:

- **Ship when the return arrives.** Safer for you, slower for the customer. Reserve the new size so it doesn't sell out in the meantime.
- **Ship right away.** Faster for the customer, but you carry the risk until the original comes back.

If the new size is out of stock, offer a refund or store credit instead, and say so plainly. If the price differs, handle the difference under your rules.

## Store credit or refund

Be clear about which one the caller will get, and if your policy allows a choice, let them pick. A refund goes back to the original payment method, and it can take a few business days to appear after you process it, depending on the bank. Store credit is usually available sooner, and some stores add a small bonus to make it the more attractive choice.

Consumer rules on refunds differ by state and country, so check what applies to you before you limit refunds to store credit.

## Final-sale items

Say no clearly and kindly, and explain why: "That item was marked final sale, so we can't take it back."

Defective or wrong items are a separate path. A final-sale dress with a broken zipper is a quality problem and goes to whoever handles those. A voice agent should never make exceptions to final sale on its own. Exceptions are a person's call.

## What to escalate

- items that arrived damaged, defective or wrong, when your rules say a person decides the fix
- requests outside the return window, when the caller asks for an exception
- final-sale items the caller wants to return anyway
- a return marked received with no refund issued yet
- callers who fail verification, or patterns that look like return fraud
- an upset caller, or a repeat call about the same return
