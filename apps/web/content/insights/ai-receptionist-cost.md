AI receptionist cost usually comes from one of four pricing models: per minute, per call, a flat monthly fee, or a plan bundled with your point of sale. The sticker price matters less than two numbers you can work out yourself: what each booked call costs you, and what each missed call costs you today. The examples below show how to run both with your own figures.

## The four pricing models

### Per minute

You pay for talk time. This is simple to understand and can be cheap at low volume. It gets expensive when calls run long, when you have a lot of spam calls, or when a busy month doubles your minutes. Ask how minutes are rounded. Rounding every call up to the next full minute adds up.

### Per call

You pay a set amount for each answered call, whatever its length. This is easier to budget. Ask how spam, hang-ups and wrong numbers are counted, and whether a transferred call counts once or twice.

### Flat monthly

One price covers a set amount of calls, or unlimited calls. This is the easiest to budget and removes any reason to hesitate before forwarding more calls to it. Check what "unlimited" excludes, such as extra locations or extra phone numbers.

### Bundled with a POS

Some systems include the AI receptionist in a point-of-sale plan, so the phone, calendar and checkout share one customer record. ## What pushes the price up

- Setup or onboarding fees
- Integrations with a calendar, POS or practice system you already use
- Extra locations or phone numbers
- Additional languages
- Live human backup for transfers
- Text message fees for confirmations and reminders
- Overage charges past a call or minute cap
- Annual contracts with early cancellation fees

None of these are wrong to charge for. You just want them in the quote, in writing, before you compare.

## Example: estimate your monthly usage

Pull a month of call history from your phone provider. Then do the arithmetic. As an example:

- 25 calls a day, open 26 days a month: 25 × 26 = 650 calls
- Average call length of 2.5 minutes: 650 × 2.5 = 1,625 minutes

On a per-minute plan at an example rate of $0.40, that is $650 a month. On a per-call plan at an example rate of $1.50, it is $975. On a flat plan, it is the flat price. These rates are made up for the exercise. Plug in the real ones from each quote.

## Example: what a missed call costs you now

This is the other side of the ledger. Say:

- You miss 8 calls a week
- 1 in 4 of those callers wanted to book
- Your average ticket is $70

That is 8 × 0.25 × $70 = $140 a week, or about $606 a month at 4.33 weeks per month. It does not count lifetime value: a new client who comes back every six weeks is worth far more than one ticket. Our guide to [missed calls for small businesses](/knowledge-base/missed-calls) shows how to measure your real miss rate.

## Cost per booked call: the number to compare

Divide what you pay by what it books. Say a plan costs $300 a month and books 90 appointments or orders that would otherwise have gone to voicemail or a callback. That is $300 ÷ 90, or about $3.33 per booking.

Compare that to your average ticket. If the cost per booking is a small fraction of what a booking brings in, the math works. If it is close to your margin, look for a cheaper model or check whether the system is really finishing calls or just taking messages. That second problem is the main gap between an AI receptionist and a [traditional phone answering service](/insights/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service).

## Compared with hiring

A person at the front desk does far more than answer phones, so this is not a straight swap. It helps to see the coverage side by side. As an example, a part-time receptionist working 20 hours a week at $18 an hour costs $360 a week before payroll taxes, or about $1,560 a month. That buys 20 hours of phone coverage out of the 168 hours in a week.

Many businesses keep the person and let the AI take overflow, after-hours calls and the rush, so staff can focus on the people standing in front of them.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. Is this per minute, per call, flat, or bundled? How are spam and hang-ups counted?
2. What does setup cost, and how long does it take?
3. Does it book into my live calendar, or does it just send me notes?
4. Are text confirmations included?
5. Is it month to month? What does it cost to leave?
6. Can I add locations or languages later, and at what price?
