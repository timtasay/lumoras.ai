The best appointment reminder texts go out twice: once a day or two ahead with an easy way to confirm or reschedule, and once a few hours before the visit. Each one names your business, the service, the date and time, and gives one clear action. Keep them short enough to read on a lock screen.

## When to send them

Timing depends on how far ahead people book and how much notice you need to refill a slot.

- **Salons, barbers and spas.** 48 hours ahead with confirm or reschedule, then a short note the morning of. The 48-hour text gives you time to fill a cancellation from the waitlist.
- **Clinics and dental offices.** Two to three days ahead, especially if forms are due before the visit. Then a same-day reminder with arrival instructions.
- **Restaurants.** A same-day text for reservations, a few hours before seating. Large parties may warrant a day-before confirmation as well.
- **Home services.** A day-before text with the arrival window, then an "on the way" text with an ETA when the tech leaves the last job.

A third reminder rarely helps. If two texts did not get a reply, a phone call is the better follow-up.

## What to put in every reminder

1. **Who you are.** The business name, first. People get a lot of texts.
2. **What and when.** Service, date, time. Add the staff member's name if clients book by person.
3. **One action.** Reply C to confirm, R to reschedule. One instruction beats a paragraph.
4. **What to bring or do.** Forms, parking, "arrive 10 minutes early." Only if it matters.
5. **How to opt out.** In the US, include a line like "Reply STOP to opt out."

A standard text message holds 160 plain characters before it splits into two. Emoji and some special characters shrink that limit, so test your template before you schedule it.

## Templates you can copy

Swap in your own details in the brackets.

**Salon, 48 hours ahead**
> [Salon]: Hi [Name], you're booked for [service] with [stylist] on [Thu] at [10:00]. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule. Reply STOP to opt out.

**Clinic, 3 days ahead**
> [Clinic]: Reminder of your visit on [Tue, Oct 14] at [9:00]. Please complete your forms before you arrive: [link]. Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule.

**Restaurant, day of**
> [Restaurant]: Your table for [4] is set for tonight at [7:30]. Running late or need to change? Reply here and we'll sort it out.

**Home services, day before**
> [Company]: Your tech visit is tomorrow between [8:00 and 10:00]. We'll text when they're on the way. Reply R to reschedule.

**Same-day nudge**
> [Business]: See you today at [2:15], [Name]. Reply R if you need to move it.

## Two-way texts do the real work

A reminder that cannot take a reply asks the client to call you, and if they call while you are busy, the cancellation reaches you too late or never. Two-way reminders let the client reply "R" and pick a new time, and the freed slot goes straight back on the calendar.

This is why reminders and your phone line belong together. ## Example: what a reminder can be worth

The value of reminders comes from early notice. Here is the arithmetic, as an example:

- 160 appointments a month
- Reminders surface 6 cancellations with at least a day's notice
- You refill 4 of those from a waitlist
- Average ticket of $70

That is 4 × $70 = $280 a month that would otherwise have been an empty chair or an idle tech. Your numbers will differ. Track confirms, reschedules and refills for a month to find out.

## Consent and compliance basics

This is general guidance for US businesses, not legal advice. Confirm the details with your texting provider.

- Get the client's permission to text when they book, and keep a record of it.
- Send reminders about the appointment only. Promotions usually need separate consent.
- Honor STOP replies right away.
- Avoid sending late at night or very early in the morning.
- Business texting from local numbers in the US generally requires registration with the carriers, often called A2P 10DLC. Most providers handle this for you.
- Clinics should keep clinical details out of texts. "Your visit on Tuesday" is enough.

## Common mistakes

- Sending from a number nobody monitors, so replies vanish
- No business name, so the text looks like spam
- Reminders that go out after the cancellation window has already closed
- Different wording at every location
- Texting a link to a login page for a simple confirm
